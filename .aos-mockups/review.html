Room to grow.
Keep the heart simple.

These ideas have a home inside the core screens. They don’t need to compete with people, plans, and gifts in the main navigation.

Notice and remember

Inspiration boards belong in a person’s Ideas tab. Phone and browser sharing feed a quick-capture sheet, with a recipient and occasion added when useful.

Choose thoughtfully

Suggestions and price watch belong inside a gift. Suggested ideas should say why they fit and let a person decide what to save.

Coordinate with care

Calendar connections belong in plan details. Surprise-safe sharing belongs at audience selection before anything is shared. Existing family visibility alone is not a surprise guarantee.

The app as it exists

The whole current screen set.

Sign-in and registration; the dashboard; recipients, profile, and add-person form; occasions, plan, and add-occasion form; gift catalog, detail, add, and edit; lists, buying board, and create-list form; assignments; and field-option settings. Card-based detail overlays add person preferences, budgets, linked records, comments, and history; gift and occasion overlays use the same pattern. Supporting dialogs handle quick add, buyer assignment, linking, price and image edits, groups, shared gifts, and field-option editing. The entry page redirects to the dashboard.

There are also separate animation, query, styling, and component playground pages for development. They are listed in the source inventory, outside the family’s everyday flow.

The current occasion and list-board phone views overflow horizontally. Saving an occasion budget returned a server error even though the value later appeared. Explicit buyer assignment succeeded, while the current assignments screen remained empty. Those are existing app behaviors shown in these captures.

Sources and verification

Captured from the checked-out app on November fourteenth. Only fictional records were created through its API. A fresh isolated PostgreSQL cluster was migrated, then its missing activity table recreated from the existing model. No production source edit or family database connection was used. Development-tool buttons remain visible in the real-app captures.

Current app fonts were cached and fulfilled locally during capture; all other external browser requests were blocked. Proposed images were made through native Codex image generation, then visually inspected. The review page and prototype need no network requests.

Page colors and typography follow the admission page: paper, forest ink, rust, honey, sage; Optima and Trebuchet for interface text, Baskerville and Georgia for headings. The app concepts draw from the paper register and Apple’s content-first controls. Illustration directions use cut paper, a family workshop, and a field journal. No canonical family likenesses were used; the brief’s fictional demo family takes precedence.
